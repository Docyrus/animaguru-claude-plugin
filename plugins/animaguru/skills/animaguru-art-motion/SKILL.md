# AnimaGuru Art Motion

Build an original film by choosing how its world is made and how its ideas move. This is one skill with 36 art-style references and nine explainer grammars, adapted into English from Huashu Art Motion. The references are production guidance, not 45 installed renderers or downloadable templates.

## Choose the treatment

Reuse the user's subject, style, brand, reference, language, format and existing authorization. In an AnimaGuru video workspace, follow the sibling `animaguru-video` production contract and reuse its completed brief. An explicit request for this skill is a style choice; do not ask the user to choose it again from the gallery.

- For a material, painting movement or animated art world, open [the art-style index](references/art-styles.md), then only the chosen card.
- For an explanation, open [the motion-grammar index](references/explainer-grammars.md), then the card matching the passage's explanatory job.
- For a style journey, keep subject identity and action continuous while changing materials. The same-scene method is in [production](references/production.md).
- For AnimaGuru's established tactile editorial identity, use sibling `animaguru-cutcraft` when present; [editorial evidence](references/explainers/editorial-evidence.md) supplies document and annotation choreography.

Material and grammar can combine: a mineral-painted world may explain a process through a concept morph; a product film may alternate actual interface footage with a restrained paper diagram. Do not add a grammar that weakens the content simply to display another technique.

## Make the film

Read [production and generation](references/production.md) before producing assets. Pair each narrated idea with a visible event, identify authentic evidence, and establish a strong subject-specific still. Choose procedural drawing, generated layers, generated video or a hybrid according to the work. Use AnimaGuru's actual available models, estimates, asset IDs and jobs; keep essential type and logos exact in the composition.

Read [rendering and review](references/rendering.md) when implementing scenes. Reuse the installed player, scene kit, cue sheet, audio tools and exporter. For PixiJS code, read the available `pixijs` skill first. If the runtime or tools are absent, inspect the project and available capabilities before choosing a local implementation; do not assume upstream `PAINT`, `CAM`, `DG` or `MO` globals are installed.

Motion should serve the selected treatment. An animated painting may have a main action and a few material-specific loops; an evidence passage may hold completely still while a sentence is read. Move the camera to reveal a relation or detail, then let it settle. Repeated slow pushes and repeated fast pushes both become mechanical.

Keep timing seekable, variation seeded and media local. Review the actual film at normal speed, inspect transitions and extreme poses, and verify the export. Report only checks performed. Creation/export does not imply publication or a new paid generation budget.

## Reference use and attribution

The English cards preserve useful construction methods, timing examples, motif motion and failure cases. Source measurements describe upstream experiments; they do not certify a new AnimaGuru implementation. Adapt pixel values to the actual format, English reading pace and brief.

Read [sources and scope](references/sources.md) when extending or redistributing the skill; [coverage.json](references/coverage.json) maps all 45 cards to the pinned source revision. Retain the included [MIT notice](LICENSE). Demo likenesses, third-party mascots, fonts and footage are not included. Use original or authorized assets.
