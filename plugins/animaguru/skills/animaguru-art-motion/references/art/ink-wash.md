# Ink wash and dry brush

Paper, expressive ink, bleed and meaningful unpainted space.

## Material and construction

Begin with paper, then add ink; do not fill the entire image before adding a paper filter. Sample strokes by arc length with a tapered brush tip, bristle gaps and dry-brush white breaks. Add a softly blurred ink-bleed layer locally. Use restrained seals or calligraphy only when appropriate, with meaningful text composed separately.

## Palette

Warm paper, black and diluted gray ink, with an optional small vermilion seal.

## Motion

Write a stroke into existence, let ink spread after contact, and animate a leaf, shrimp antenna or rippling water. Keep dry-brush gaps attached to the stroke instead of flickering independently.

## Transition

A brush stroke writes the next frame or a controlled ink wash spreads.

## Failure cases and adaptation

Do not import an oil-paint layer order. Large negative space is part of the medium. The source figures were less expressive than its brushwork: prepare a convincing subject still and apply the same ink edge treatment to it.
