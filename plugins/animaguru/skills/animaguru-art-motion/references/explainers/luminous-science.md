# Luminous flat science worlds

Choose a visual metaphor before drawing: what physical thing represents this abstract idea? Use this grammar for systems, processes and journeys across scale. Authentic archives or real events usually benefit more from editorial evidence.

## Visual structure

Use clear organic/geometric silhouettes, usually without dark outlines. Shade with a darker local hue and a small highlight shape. A dark ground with two or three contrasting saturated accents can support luminous signals; a lighter treatment is also possible. Choose a coherent era/palette rather than mixing visual systems. A glowing orange central planet is one source example, not a required subject. Plan foreground, subject, landscape and distant layers with meaningful depth.

## Motion and timing

Each explanatory passage should have a visible state change: a connection forms, a structure opens, an object divides or a level is revealed. Once settled, labels and subjects hold; stars, clouds or particles may continue quietly. Source references use approximately .83 s for a regular action, faster .17–.5 s for selected pops and a short stagger between related objects. These are study values, not quotas. Avoid making every object float to satisfy a motion metric.

## Camera and transitions

Identify and briefly highlight the target before entering it. Interpolate large scale changes in logarithmic space. Keep the world live and redraw vectors or use sufficient-resolution source assets: magnifying a 1920 px screenshot 60 times will blur. Match color, highlight direction and object position across a portal; pull back to reveal context. Scale travel can take around 1–2 s, while whole conceptual units often need several seconds of narration.

## AnimaGuru adaptation and review

Use original subjects and brand-appropriate colors; use generated transparent layers when character drawing is the bottleneck. Apply compatible shading/material to all subjects. Existing Pixi containers provide depth stacks; only implement parallax where it explains space. Review portal coverage, object identity, readable label holds, particle density and whether the visual actually explains the concept. Short upstream demos are compressed catalogs of techniques, not timing templates for a finished science film.
