# Product and interface presentation

Use for a real interface, feature launch, product name, price or specification. Let a screenshot, recording or product image carry the frame. For mechanism, evidence or time-series data, use the corresponding grammar instead.

## Visual structure

Show one product fact at a time. A screenshot can occupy about 80% of a landscape frame or 90% of a portrait frame, then the camera finds the relevant detail. Use the product’s brand palette. Near-black grounds, generous whitespace, tight large type and concentric corner radii are useful options. Light meshes, glass cards, gradient text and decorative floating are optional packaging; stacking all of them produces a generic launch template.

## Motion and timing

Start UI springs at critical damping or very low overshoot. Source duration/bounce examples use .5–.75 s and bounce 0–.15; stronger .3 bounce is an intentional choice. An optional text reveal removes 18–26 px blur while moving 16–40 px over .7–1 s. Count a quantity only once, use tabular digits, and time its final value to the spoken number. Compute the start by subtracting the animation duration from that cue. Let units fit their measured width.

## Camera and transitions

Hold while the screen is readable. Push toward a detail around .9 s, move to another region around .7 s, then return to context. Reserve a much faster push for a real reveal. A shared-element card can expand into a full view: place the incoming opaque image inside the card from the first transition frame so old and new text do not double. Pad blur/shrink transitions with overscan to prevent dark edges.

## AnimaGuru adaptation and review

Use actual local product captures and editable exact type; label conceptual UI in production records. Keep one coordinate system between full view and detail view. A numeric conclusion about the screen can overlay that same dimmed screen instead of cutting to an unrelated gradient background. Check glass against actual content, spring restraint, glyph coverage, screenshot legibility, final holds, and accidental repeats of the same three-card layout.
