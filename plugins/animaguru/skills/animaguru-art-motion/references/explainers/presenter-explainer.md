# Presenter-led business explainer

An original presenter connects paper, charts, key terms and emotional reactions. Use for business history, product mechanisms and knowledge explainers with a visible guide. This is the ninth upstream grammar; it has a reference film but no standalone parameterized snippet or supplied character sprites.

## Visual structure

Allocate varied layouts: a presenter beside evidence, a full-frame number, a left/right comparison, a character inside a chart and a reaction close-up. Avoid a whole film of person-left/chart-right. Prepare full-body and bust views. Sitting, leaning and walking require matching pose material rather than distorting a standing frame.

## Motion and timing

Use explicit position and scale at the first character keyframe of each segment, so state does not leak from the previous segment. Normalize apparent pose height using measured anchors. Switch different silhouettes directly with a slight motivated pop instead of crossfading a double figure. Stop mouths during pauses; use complete gait frames for walking. Numbers settle on the spoken value, then the emphasis circle appears. Do not attach “over” or “+” while a count is still passing through lower values.

## Camera and transitions

Page transitions may move the page while the presenter and year display continue across it. Derive year, subtitles, chapter title and graphics from one timeline. Prefer a live scene camera to enlarging a full-frame raster image; keep the presenter inside the actual viewport at every zoom.

## AnimaGuru adaptation and review

Create or import an authorized original presenter through AnimaGuru and save local frames with actual bounds and foot anchors. Reuse the player’s captions, audio cues and scene containers. Multiply nested opacity by the parent value rather than replacing it, preserving outer fades. Check text occlusion, subtitle-zone conflicts, character cropping, matching year labels and numerical qualifiers. The source card is a production recipe, not a complete reusable film or proof that arbitrary presenter performance is supported.
