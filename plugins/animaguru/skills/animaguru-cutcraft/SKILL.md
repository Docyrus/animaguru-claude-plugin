# AnimaGuru Cutcraft

Make an original editorial film that explains an idea through things the viewer can see move, connect, separate and resolve. Use AnimaGuru for generation and a controlled composition for titles, labels, timing and branding. The style can serve another product; use that product's facts and identity.

## Establish the film

Reuse the user's subject, references, language, placement and generation authorization. Resolve only consequential missing choices. For an exploratory sample, state sensible assumptions and proceed: a short English landscape film with a single narrator is a useful starting point, not a requirement for every film.

Read first-party product material before writing claims. Separate actual product footage from conceptual illustrations in the production records. Avoid unverified performance claims, invented customer evidence, and promises of perfect AI results. End an advertisement with a relevant invitation; end an educational explainer on its insight unless a CTA was requested.

Write a concise narration and a beat sheet pairing each spoken idea with a visible event. Duration follows the measured voice and the user's brief. A problem, a concrete mechanism, an outcome and a short close can work for a product film; choose the structure that fits the subject.

## The Cutcraft visual language

- **Material:** matte colored cardstock, visible fibers, slightly irregular scissor edges and short directional contact shadows. Layer flat pieces; keep silhouettes readable. Avoid glossy plastic and unrelated photorealistic environments.
- **AnimaGuru palette:** warm cream `#F7F2E8`, ink `#162B38`, brand blue `#2C6BD0`, orange `#E67D43`, with restrained sage `#93AA8B`. For client films, replace accents with the client's brand. Backgrounds should have quiet areas for type.
- **Typography:** one bold editorial heading family and a compatible reading face. Use actual local fonts, vector logos and rendered text for names, numbers and claims. Keep generated footage free of essential lettering. Short labels summarize; narration carries the full sentence.
- **Composition:** one dominant idea per beat, useful negative space, visibly separated foreground and background. A print-like grid, small folio marks and a fine blue connector line give the film its own identity. Avoid copying Vox's logo, layouts or other identifying brand elements.
- **Motion:** cutouts slide, rotate at plausible paper joints, pin into place, peel, stack or reveal. A connector is drawn when a relationship becomes clear. Motion answers the spoken line; texture and camera movement remain restrained.
- **Continuity:** lock material, light direction, palette, camera language and recurring character description. An orange fox with a cobalt scarf is an optional AnimaGuru motif, not compulsory casting for other subjects.
- **Sound:** one narrator across the film, a quiet instrumental bed and a few motivated paper taps or rustles. Keep speech intelligible and let the closing thought breathe.

Build a visual explanation instead of using the same decorative collage under every sentence. If illustrating control, visibly move one object while its surroundings stay fixed. If explaining a connected workflow, show the pieces joining in the order the narration names them.

## Generate with AnimaGuru

Read [the generation adapter](references/generation.md) when producing media. It covers CLI discovery, supported references, credit estimates, continuity and local assembly. Do not substitute MaxFusion calls or assume a Google Omni model exists.

Prefer the installed AnimaGuru CLI for local asset work; use connected MCP tools when they are available and appropriate. Creating this skill is not generation authorization. A user request to generate the film authorizes a proportionate initial attempt; honor stated budgets and surface a concrete estimate before submission. Do not purchase credits or change provider defaults as part of film production.

Generate artwork and motion separately from exact type. For a short trial, a few strong compositions with changing editorial labels usually communicate more than many loosely related clips. Reuse good existing textures, music or SFX when they fit. A generated clip may be one layer inside a controlled film rather than the entire frame.

For precise placement or when video providers are unavailable, generate isolated transparent paper subjects and animate their transforms in the composition. Build the fixed setting from separate paper layers. This is a complete production route: character movement, path reveals, clip placement and typography can remain editable without a video-model call. Describe it as animation of generated artwork, not as AI-generated video footage.

Use a single speech asset or consistently configured segments. Measure the recording before finalizing scenes; do not assume the requested pacing was achieved. Replace or mute incidental speech from video generation when using the separate narrator.

## Review and deliver

Keep the brief, narration, scene timings, request settings, estimates, job IDs, returned asset IDs, costs and source links together with the film's production files. Preserve an editable composition or reproducible render recipe and local copies of the media.

Inspect the actual opening, middle, transitions and closing frames. Check exact brand spelling, readable labels, character consistency, contrast, crop and any model artifacts. Check audio duration and clipping; listen when playback tools permit, and describe listening limitations honestly. Repair the composition before buying another generation when timing, crop or typography can solve the problem.

Export a playable local film and report what was generated, what was reused, and any remaining defects. Return a preview, the final file, and a link to the skill. Creating a film does not imply publishing it publicly or changing the product website.
