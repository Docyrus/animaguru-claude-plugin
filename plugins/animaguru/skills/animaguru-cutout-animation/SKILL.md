# Cutout Animation

This template defines the visual treatment and animation language: separated silhouettes, layered composition, material edges, depth, and purposeful movement. It works with Explainer, Advertisement and General Video projects. The subject, audience, tone, message, narrative structure, duration, language, and call to action come from the user's brief.

## Establish the brief

For a new film, **follow the category-then-style selection flow and quick interview in the sibling `animaguru-video` skill before production**. This skill being installed does not mean the user has selected it. Share the [animation-style gallery](https://animaguru.com/animation-styles#cutout-animation) when the user wants to see this style. Reuse supplied choices and completed briefs; honor an explicit request to skip the interview. Include the visual choices below in the brief interview when they are still open:

- Cutout material: photography, illustration, printed paper, mixed-media collage, or a supplied reference.
- Composition: graphic and flat, or layered with visible depth, shadows and parallax.
- Motion: smooth editorial movement, articulated cutout animation, or stepped stop-motion cadence.
- Visual constraints: brand palette, typography, texture, edge finish, characters or objects, and required asset references.

A compact default direction can combine clean silhouettes, overlapping planes and eased 2D movement. Derive casting, mood, colors, tempo and scene design from the brief. Do not infer an audience, relationship between characters, story arc, fixed scene count, narration style or CTA from this template.

## Apply the style

Read [visual and motion guidance](references/visual-style.md) when designing scenes. Read [the production recipe](references/production-recipe.md) when generating and integrating cutout assets. Follow the sibling `animaguru-video` skill for the interview, source research, production records, resource timeline, audio and export workflow.

Use image tools for bitmap artwork, cutouts and textures, and PixiJS for composition, typography, masks and motion. Treat people, products, objects, landscapes and abstract forms as equally valid cutout subjects. Choose coherent edge treatment, material, lighting and movement so the film feels intentionally assembled.

Use the user's selected generation models and budget. Otherwise inspect the current AnimaGuru catalog and configured providers to select models suited to the assets and motion. MiniMax H3 Max (`fal/minimax-h3-max`) or its faster, cheaper Turbo (`fal/minimax-h3-max-turbo`) is one option for animated photographic assets when available; cutout motion can also be built directly in PixiJS from still layers. Validate supported settings before submission.

Deliver the film described in the brief with consistent cutout visuals and intentional animation. Keep story development responsive to the project's purpose rather than borrowing the narrative of an example film.
