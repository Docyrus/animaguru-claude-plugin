# Cutout visual and motion language

Use these techniques to realize the user's subject and message. Select a coherent subset for the film; its narrative order and pacing come from the brief.

## Material and silhouette

- Photography gives subjects recognizable surface detail; illustration permits simplified forms; printed paper adds a tactile finish. Combine them when the contrast is deliberate.
- Choose crisp, torn, outlined or visibly handmade edges. Keep edge quality consistent across assets and inspect fine detail at the intended viewing size.
- Separate foreground subjects, props and background planes. Leave sufficient margins for movement and use clean transparency or a controlled key color.
- Match lighting, perspective and scale across photographic assets. Derive colors, type and textures from the project's identity and visual references.
- Apply grain and paper texture selectively so they support the material without obscuring subjects or type.

## Composition and depth

- Use overlap, restrained shadows and differing layer scales to establish depth. Keep contact shadows anchored to the relevant surface.
- Frame people, objects, products, diagrams or abstract shapes according to the scene's purpose. A human cast is optional.
- Use negative space and clear visual hierarchy. Keep important imagery and type inside the output format's safe area, including caption space when captions are part of the brief.
- Choose layouts for the actual aspect ratio. Recompose for portrait or square rather than compressing a landscape composition.
- Keep essential type in PixiJS so it stays editable, legible and localizable. Generated artwork should not carry critical wording.

## Movement

- **Editorial motion:** use eased translation, rotation and scale, with restrained overshoot or settling when appropriate.
- **Articulated cutouts:** separate moving parts, place pivots at plausible joints or hinges, and layer overlaps to conceal seams. Preserve silhouette and material consistency during movement.
- **Stepped motion:** sample transforms at a deliberate cadence for a stop-motion feel. Keep the main timeline deterministic and choose the cadence to suit the visual reference.
- **Parallax:** move foreground and background planes at different rates. Keep camera movement controlled and relevant to what the viewer should notice.
- **Asset performance:** use generated video when nuanced gestures or organic motion are needed. Animate still layers in Pixi when transforms, reveals or articulation can achieve the result more precisely.
- **Transitions:** slide, layer, peel, reveal, occlude or match shapes in ways that suit the material. Use a consistent transition vocabulary.

Give movements clear beginnings, readable holds and resolved endings. Choose energy and rhythm from the brief; the cutout style supports quiet, playful, technical, dramatic and many other treatments. Avoid perpetual motion that competes with the intended focus.

## Visual review

Inspect silhouette edges, key spill, joints, shadows, overlaps, texture scale, type fit and repeated-motion seams. Check transitions and the first/last frames at full resolution. Preview the work in its intended aspect ratio and ensure movement continues to communicate when viewed at a smaller size.
